# Finite-Red native-return gap diagnosis

Date: 2026-10-15. Branch: `blue/finite-red-check`, based on `2f44db1`.

## Question and evidence

The six native-reward actor runs underperformed Lancer by 30.95 mean points per
episode. This trace audit asks whether that gap follows from the age-80 guard or
from the learned residual on top of the guard. It reads existing Blue traces and
their separate privileged evaluation labels; labels are not available to policy
code. It checks that every logged native reward equals the sum of event penalties
and that each trace agrees with its report.

A guard-only control was evaluated on the same 24 finite-Red seeds (7665-7688),
400-step horizon, and current restored evaluator. The four reports and traces are
ignored artifacts under
`blue/results/finite_red_native_diagnosis/guard/`; the summary JSON is
`blue/results/finite_red_native_diagnosis/diagnosis.json`. The audit is
reproducible with `blue.training.harness_finite_diagnose`.

| Arm | Mean native return | Paired difference |
|---|---:|---:|
| Lancer | -151.79 | reference |
| Guard only (Lancer rules + age-80 guard) | -150.21 | +1.58 vs Lancer over the fixed episode block |
| PPO actor s0-s5 | -197.21 to -165.75 | actor-minus-Lancer mean by training seed: -45.42, -13.96, -10.08, -22.12, -25.17, -68.96 |

The actor-minus-guard mean paired differences by training seed were
`[-47.00, -15.54, -11.67, -23.71, -26.75, -70.54]`. The mean was -32.53 with
a training-seed 95% t interval `[-55.95, -9.12]`. The actor-minus-Lancer interval
remains `[-54.37, -7.54]`. These intervals use training seeds as the replication
unit and are exploratory, conditional on this fixed evaluation block. The
guard-only result is essentially level with Lancer here, so the measured loss is
associated with the learned residual rather than explained by adding the guard
alone.

## Where the return difference appears

The native return event accounting is exact. Averaged across the six actors and
the paired evaluation seeds, actor minus guard-only event penalties were:

| Native penalty | Mean difference per episode | Direction |
|---|---:|---|
| Green Local Work failure (LWF) | -22.33 | worse for actors; negative in all six training seeds |
| Red Impact/Access (RIA) | -6.72 | worse on average, but inconsistent across training seeds |
| Blue action cost | -3.49 | worse on average, close to zero across training seeds |

The increase in LWF penalties accounts for most of the mean return gap. Actor
episodes had similar privileged compromised-host exposure to guard-only: about
10,023 vs 10,048 compromised host-ticks per episode when averaging the six
actors, and about 54.0 vs 54.1 newly compromised hosts. Exposure was not
consistently lower across actor seeds. Confirmed Analyse completions ranged from
64.7 to 77.2 per episode for actors, versus 67.5 for guard-only; more
confirmations did not reliably translate into a higher native return.

The excess LWF penalty is concentrated in mission phase 1: actor-minus-guard
averaged -16.35 points per episode in phase 1, versus -2.62 in phase 0 and
-3.35 in phase 2. The phase-1 work-failure events were then broken down by
subnet and joined to Blue's same-host action history.

The phase-1 LWF increase localizes mainly to Operational Zone A. Actor-minus-
guard penalty differences per episode were -13.06 in Operational Zone A,
-2.14 in Operational Zone B, and -1.54 in Restricted Zone B. In Operational
Zone A, the six actors averaged 2.51 LWF events per episode versus 1.21 for
guard-only. Privileged labels marked the affected hosts compromised at event
time. In 1.75 actor events per episode, the last completed same-host Analyse
was `UNKNOWN` more than 20 ticks earlier, with no same-host action or pending
remediation at the event tick; this context occurred 0.88 times per episode
under guard-only. `UNKNOWN` is the Blue tracker state after Analyse, not a
clean-host finding. The actor and guard also differed in a smaller number of
events immediately following confirmed scans or while remediation was pending.
This is consistent with delayed or ineffective response in that zone, but the
traces do not establish that as the cause: the policies enter different world
states, and LWF events are generated by Green actions.

The actors intervene on the sweep investigation choice; urgent remediation and
verification remain heuristic rules. Across the six actor seeds, five issued
fewer Analyse requests and more Restore requests than guard-only. The PPO
training records also show only 0.21-0.48 greedy agreement with the Lancer
teacher in the last eight iterations, alongside 0.85-0.87 sampled override
rates. Native training return improved from the first to last eight iterations
in only three seeds and declined in the other three. This is consistent with
unstable or poorly aligned residual learning, but does not establish a cause.

## Limits and next experiment

These are associations from aggregate traces. The actor and guard trajectories
diverge once they choose different hosts, so event penalties cannot identify a
causal harmful action. Similar host exposure does not rule out service-specific
or timing effects. The exact temporary source snapshot used for training was
removed; its recorded source hashes cannot be rechecked against that snapshot.
The guard-only run uses restored files at the same base commit; `wrapper.py`,
`policy.py`, and `ordered.py` hashes match the saved actor evaluation metadata,
but the evaluator and training files do not. Treat the matched-policy result as
a useful diagnostic, not a byte-exact rerun.

The trace audit records event phase/subnet and the latest completed same-host
Blue action, plus same-tick requests, completions and pending remediation. The
candidate-level extension is now complete (see below). The next experiment,
if pursued, should freeze one intervention for residual instability (for
example, initialization near zero residual plus a reference-policy KL check)
and compare guard-only, stochastic actor, and deployed greedy actor on a new
held-out finite-Red block. Keep native return primary and preserve LWF/RIA
tails. Do not proceed to LLM Red until that comparison has a pre-registered
decision rule.

## Candidate-choice audit, 2026-10-15

Candidate lists, Lancer base scores/ranks, actor residual scores/ranks, actor
pick, and same-state Lancer/age-guard pick were recorded for `blue_agent_1`
while replaying all six frozen actors on seeds 7665-7688. The audit used only
Blue-visible candidate features and scores. Its 144 actor episodes reproduce
all 144 stored returns exactly, and every Blue action/host pair matches the
prior trace across 57,456 ticks. This confirms the instrumentation did not
change the evaluated behavior. The report and traces are ignored under
`blue/results/finite_red_candidate_audit/`; the post-hoc join is reproducible
with `blue.training.harness_finite_candidate_audit`.

Across 17,141 actor-ranked `blue_agent_1` sweep decisions, the actor selected a
different host from Lancer on 14,774 decisions (86.2%). The selected host's
mean Lancer rank was 6.93 (rank 1 is Lancer's top choice), so the residual often
overrode Lancer's leading candidate. The age guard independently took over on
about 23 decisions per episode; those rows correctly have no actor rank because
the hook was not called.

For the 362 phase-1 Operational Zone A LWF events across the six models, the
event host appeared in a prior actor-ranked candidate list in all 362 cases.
At the most recent such decision, the actor had picked the event host 23 times
and Lancer's same-state pick was that host 55 times. The actor and Lancer
choices differed at that decision for 334 events. Mean event-host rank at that
decision was 5.00 under Lancer and 5.67 under the actor. Model-level counts are:

| Training seed | Actor/Lancer disagreement | Phase-1 Op A LWF | Actor picked event host | Lancer picked event host | Mean event-host rank, Lancer / actor |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| s0 | 85.9% | 62 | 1 | 17 | 2.77 / 4.24 |
| s1 | 83.2% | 44 | 3 | 0 | 6.25 / 5.59 |
| s2 | 87.0% | 44 | 2 | 0 | 6.00 / 5.57 |
| s3 | 84.4% | 63 | 2 | 13 | 7.00 / 5.81 |
| s4 | 88.6% | 50 | 10 | 8 | 3.38 / 4.68 |
| s5 | 88.1% | 99 | 5 | 17 | 4.93 / 7.06 |

This supports a narrower association: the actor frequently reranks sweep
candidates, but the direction around Operational Zone A LWF is inconsistent
across training seeds. s0 and s5 often demote the eventual event host; s1-s3
often rank it better than Lancer, and s4 is mixed. The 362 events are not
independent trials, and each policy's trajectory diverges after a different
choice. These comparisons do not show that a specific actor choice caused an
LWF event. They do show that the actor's residual is a large intervention on
Lancer ordering, not a small tie-break. No retraining was performed; the
finite-Red decision gate remains failed, and there is no basis to proceed to
LLM Red.
