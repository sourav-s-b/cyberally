# Policy methods for Blue specialists and managers

**Date:** 2026-10-18
**Scope:** Match established policy methods to investigation, recovery, verification, coverage, and specialist routing in the current CAGE4 harness. This is a literature review and experiment recommendation; it adds no code or performance result.

## Recommendation

Build a specialist system in stages. Keep a fixed, observable rule manager for urgent response, verification, action legality, and fallback. Keep the finite-Red BC investigator as a candidate component, not a universal winner. Use development traces to choose one second specialist. Train that specialist with a method matched to its target and data. Consider a learned manager only after its specialists are fixed and a routing problem has been measured.

The most directly relevant result is H-MARL on CAGE4: its PPO-trained investigation and recovery subpolicies under a hand-written expert master outperformed the tested decentralized and learned-master baselines in most reported cases. That is evidence for **expert-guided hierarchy**, not evidence that a learned manager or an arbitrary mix of algorithms will help our harness. Its 500-step evaluation, observation/action design and Red variants differ from our 400-step harness; scores are not directly comparable. Do not assume the main study changed the native reward: a later traffic-control ablation explicitly removes a service-access penalty. Its main comparisons do not establish a win over Lancer.

The official CAGE4 competition study says Team Lancer’s highest-scoring submission was heuristic. Separately, Team Punch reports that its best heuristic outperformed its own RL agents. These are team-specific statements, not proof about every RL agent in CAGE4. That history supports keeping strong deterministic controls in every experiment. It does not imply that learned policies cannot help a specific subtask.

## Review corrections and execution constraints

- **Baseline identity:** the local “Lancer” control is a clean-room Lancer-style policy, not a demonstrated reproduction of the original competition submission. `control_policy` in `blue/training/harness_bc_redsuite.py` constructs unguarded Lancer with `OrderedPolicy`; the guard control uses `HarnessPolicy(max_age=80)`; BC uses the same harness with its investigation hook. Treat all wins as local comparisons.
- **No identified second-specialist failure yet:** Random-Red BC−guard is −5.89 with the conditional interval [−11.53, −0.25], but this does not demonstrate a recovery or verification weakness. Audit investigation errors as well as other branches. If the only supported failure is investigation, improve that component or retain the rule investigator; do not force a second specialist.
- **Timing is part of manager authority:** a recovery specialist confined to Remove-versus-Restore under unchanged routing cannot learn when recovery is invoked. Testing timing requires a separately specified routing experiment. Runtime legality and pending-action safety remain mandatory even when task selection becomes learned.
- **Action support:** the current wrapper exposes Sleep, Monitor, and host Analyse/Remove/Restore. It does not expose decoy deployment or Block/Allow traffic. Traffic control from the paper needs an explicit wrapper/interface proposal; it is not a run-ready specialist.
- **Paper scale and uncertainty:** the H-MARL study evaluates 100 randomized 500-step episodes and reports episode standard deviations, not our model-seed confidence intervals. Its configured train buffer has one million samples and it uses 30 collection workers. A small local PPO pilot is an adaptation; measure its budget and do not call it a reproduction.
- **Role assignments are hypotheses:** the reviewed evidence does not demonstrate that PPO is uniquely best for recovery, or that using different algorithms per specialist beats a common algorithm. Keep an existing rule and a simple training baseline in comparisons. Supervised verification predictions do not identify the best recheck time without coverage of alternative timings; successful Remove is not a clean-host label.
- **Scope:** multiple neural modules form a modular policy. A manager learning from rewards is hierarchical RL when its action selects defined subpolicies/options; multiple modules alone do not establish MARL. Specify which Blue agents learn, what observations each receives, and any authorized communication. Hidden compromise state stays outside all learner inputs and training labels under the current contract.

## Role-to-method design for this harness

| Specialist / manager | Current owner | Method to consider | Why this method fits | Required evidence before training |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Investigation | Host ordering hook | Existing BC; later reward fine-tuning only as a separate RL test | This is a ranking decision with Lancer labels and a clear legal candidate set. The current BC already has a finite-Red win. | Use the completed Red-suite result to limit the claim; identify a development-only investigation failure; retain age guard and compare on fresh paired seeds. |
| Recovery | Rule branch for Remove/Restore | PPO subpolicy from fresh simulator rollouts; IQL/CRR only after offline-data repair | Delayed native return can distinguish recovery timing and action choice. The direct CAGE4 H-MARL evidence trains a PPO recovery subpolicy under expert routing. | Transition audit with action completion, duration, reward, next observation, termination flags, enough recovery examples, and no hidden compromise labels in inputs. |
| Verification | Rule branch after remediation | Keep rule; supervised predictor if a trustworthy observed target exists | This is a constrained outcome/timing question, not automatically a high-level RL problem. | Whole-episode labels for recheck result and delay; handle missing/censored outcomes; convert prediction into a policy and assess return. |
| Coverage | Age-80 guard | Keep rule | Both compared policies already have this guard. The Random-Red loss implicates changing the investigator within that shared harness; it does not isolate coverage quality. | Demonstrate a development-set gap in age, coverage, or service impact that the fixed guard misses. |
| Manager | Fixed precedence and fallback rules | PPO over frozen subpolicies/options, only after specialist validation | Direct CAGE4 evidence favors expert routing in normal cases and shows learned routing can help when the expert routing rule is uncertain. | Measured Blue-visible routing errors, frozen specialist checkpoints, option definitions/termination, valid action masks, and paired evaluation. |

Do not assign algorithms for variety's sake. A specialist can be a deterministic rule, a supervised model, an imitation policy, or an RL policy. The composed agent is judged by native team return, lower-tail failures, coverage, and service effects—not specialist accuracy alone.

## Experiment sequence

1. **C0: audit separate development traces.** For each decision branch, record the legal choices, selected and executed action, Blue-visible cues, duration, later observed outcome, and missing fields. Do not mine the held-out cross-Red block to pick a specialist or threshold.
2. **Select one second specialist.** Choose a second specialist only if C0 finds a repeated non-investigation failure with an observable signal. If the evidence instead supports an investigation correction, revise the investigator experiment and retain the other rules. Match the method to the evidence: supervised learning for reliable labels; PPO for a task with a native-reward learning signal and sufficient fresh episodes; offline IQL/CRR only for complete, adequately supported transitions.
3. **Validate the specialist alone.** Keep all other modules and the manager fixed. Compare against the current branch's rule on development seeds. Check reset, legal actions, pending-action attribution, fallback, tails, and no privileged inputs.
4. **Run the frozen four-arm composition test.** B = Lancer investigator + common harness; I = BC investigator + that same harness; S = Lancer investigator + candidate specialist; IS = both frozen specialists. For a recovery/verification specialist, all four arms use the same age-80 guard, feature pipeline and remaining rules. B is the existing guard baseline; retain unguarded Lancer, Sleep and masked-random as separate controls. For a coverage specialist, change only coverage in S/IS and retain age-80 coverage in B/I. Reuse exact component checkpoints across paired arms. Primary comparison: IS−B. Also report I−B, S−B, IS−I, IS−S and the interaction (IS−I)−(S−B). Beating B alone does not show that both specialists contribute.
5. **Only then test manager learning.** Freeze specialist weights. Compare rule routing with a PPO manager over the same options. Define routing at free-agent decision events and specify option termination and interruption by urgent work. For an option lasting d ticks, accumulate R = sum(gamma**j * r[t+j], j=0..d-1) and bootstrap with gamma**d; use matching elapsed-time handling in advantages. Evaluate undiscounted native episode return. Do not count forced waiting as fresh policy decisions. Stop if no routing error is measurable or if learned routing worsens tails or coverage.

An interval crossing zero is inconclusive. Keep the simpler component unless a specialist shows an independently useful effect and the composition passes predeclared return, tail, and coverage limits. Use at least five new training RNG replicates for a learned specialist; report whether their training datasets are shared. Five models evaluated on one common seed block do not quantify uncertainty over new scenario blocks or new training data. Supplement the conditional model-seed interval with paired episode uncertainty (resample each episode jointly across models and controls), preserve tail episodes, and confirm on an untouched block. Sleep Red is a no-attack control: require acceptable behavior there, not strict improvement over a zero-return baseline. Predeclare active-Red gains and regression tolerances; adjust or clearly label multiple exploratory claims.
