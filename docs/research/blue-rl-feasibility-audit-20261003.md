# Can learned Blue reliably beat the CAGE4 heuristics?

Research audit, 2026-10-03. Branch: `blue/mappo-training` at `a6eab7e`.
This is a source and literature review. No new policy was trained or evaluated
for this report. The earlier surveys in this directory contain useful leads;
their search-existence checks do not establish each paper's findings. The
papers linked below were checked against publisher, proceedings, author, or
official implementation sources.

## Assessment

The current evidence supports **possibility, not a reliable RL win**. In the
native 400-step regression suite, the best IQL-derived checkpoint scored
`-82.0` against round-robin's `-93.5` across eight seeds. On the eight
held-out seeds it scored `-96.0` against `-85.1`. Other IQL training seeds
were weaker. Attention-based imitation approximately matched the teacher;
MAPPO fine-tuning repeatedly lost coverage and return. These are the project
results in [proposal 14](../proposals/14-offline-stitching.md),
[proposal 02](../proposals/02-factorized-actor.md), and
[Blue status](../status/blue-session.md). None proves an impossibility of RL.

The [official CAGE4 study](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/35158/37313)
found its top heuristic submissions ahead of MARL and attacker variants hard
for all entrants. Its 100 by 500-step FiniteStateRed protocol differs from
our 400-step runs, mostly against Discovery Red. Our own
[Red-swap suite](../proposals/15-generalization-suite.md) finds finite Red
substantially harder than discovery on the same eight seeds. Therefore the
previous local claim that base-scenario reward was already in the official
winner's band is not supported by a matched comparison.

The strongest opportunity is to improve the defender's **host ordering** while
retaining the heuristic's broad investigation and sound remediation behavior.
The repository has not yet tested that opportunity with a canonical offline
learner, a policy whose recurrent context can change same-command host
ordering, or a valid offline policy evaluator. Those are more immediate than
a larger GNN or another long MAPPO run.

## Findings that change how earlier experiments should be read

1. **The current FQE selector is not FQE.** [Le et al., ICML 2019, Algorithm
   3](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/le19a/le19a.pdf) fits the value of
   the *logged* action: `Q(s_t, a_t) <- r_t + gamma Q(s_{t+1}, pi(s_{t+1}))`.
   [`fit_fqe`](../../blue/blue_fqe_select.py) receives logged `A` but never
   uses it; its regression and residual gather `pi_S` on the left. It assigns
   rewards generated by one action to a potentially different candidate
   action. The policy is also scored with zero GRU memory at every transition
   and without the deployment action mask. Its `gamma=0.99` predictions are
   compared with an undiscounted Monte Carlo anchor. The six close FQE
   scores in `fqe-v3-20261003.json` **do not establish equal policy values**;
   that conclusion in the latest session note must be treated as retracted.
   Even a repaired FQE would face difficult support, partial observation,
   and changing-teammate dynamics with these local observations and shared
   team rewards. Two network-fit seeds cannot measure those errors.

2. **The IQL target network is unused.** [`blue_iql.py`](../../blue/blue_iql.py)
   creates and slowly updates `qt`, but its value update and actor extraction
   read the online `q`. The [official IQL learner](https://github.com/ikostrikov/implicit_q_learning/blob/master/learner.py)
   uses a target critic for both; its [critic](https://github.com/ikostrikov/implicit_q_learning/blob/master/critic.py)
   uses the minimum of two Q estimates. The local single-Q implementation
   can still be a legitimate ablation, but the existing runs are not evidence
   about canonical IQL stability. [The IQL paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06169)
   supports in-dataset policy improvement, not a claim that this adaptation
   must beat the CAGE4 heuristic.

3. **Offline transitions lose the final action's reward and mislabel the end.**
   [`blue_collect_bc.py`](../../blue/blue_collect_bc.py) records the observation
   before each action and its reward after that action, with no final
   post-action observation. [`blue_iql.py`](../../blue/blue_iql.py) and
   [`blue_fqe_select.py`](../../blue/blue_fqe_select.py) build transitions only
   through `obs[:-1]`, drop the last action/reward, and mark the preceding
   transition terminal even though `obs[-1]` is its real next observation.
   A new collector should store the final observation and the actual
   `terminated`/`truncated` flags. A compatibility conversion of old logs
   should explicitly record that their final reward cannot become a normal
   `(s,a,r,s')` transition without a stored `s'`.

4. **Offline extraction optimizes a different action distribution.** The
   policy step in [`blue_iql.py`](../../blue/blue_iql.py) computes cross
   entropy over raw 155-action logits. Deployment chooses only among valid
   actions. Loss on free ticks is applied, but invalid action logits still
   compete in the training softmax. Mask logits with the saved simulator
   validity mask before cross entropy; leave evidence-based restrictions
   separate. Train/validation episode splits and checkpoint selection are
   also absent: extraction runs a fixed 30 epochs. Scaling rewards by 100
   while keeping `w=exp(beta * advantage)` with the same `beta` weakens
   weight contrast mechanically. Lower scaled Q loss and small advantage
   spread do not establish a more useful policy.

5. **The actor's memory cannot change same-command host ordering.** In
   [`FactorizedRNNAgent`](../../blue/blue_factorized_agent.py), each host
   score and command score is a *linear* function of `[slot_embedding,
   shared_GRU_context]`. For any command `c` and hosts `s,t`, the shared
   context term cancels in `logit(s,c) - logit(t,c)`. The current slot
   attention also receives no recurrent context and no presence mask for
   padded entities. This is an algebraic limitation of that path, not proof
   that it caused every observed failure. The flat softmax of
   `host_score + command_score` is also not an independently normalized
   autoregressive host-then-command distribution: command `logsumexp`
   changes its host marginal.

6. **A frozen baseline does not automatically guarantee a return floor.**
   A learned selector can choose a poor action even if the baseline actor is
   frozen. [SPIBB](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/laroche19a.html) obtains
   safety claims only under its explicit data, uncertainty, and finite-MDP
   assumptions; a deep CAGE4 fallback has no inherited theorem. Proposal
   16's PEX wording should be read as protection from overwriting base
   weights, not a performance guarantee. Preserve a measurable investigation
   capability and test return and bad tails empirically.

### Why this order

The first four experiments attack identified interpretation and expressivity
problems with small changes. They can succeed or fail without a costly new
multiagent training stack. If teacher parity becomes stable, there is a
credible opening for constrained online improvement. A large Transformer,
full GNN, HAPPO, world model, or POMCP baseline adds complexity before we
know whether the corrected action/data path works. [GTrXL](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/parisotto20a.html)
is useful if memory ablation shows a capacity problem, but cannot make a
purely additive recurrent term alter relative host scores. [AWAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09359)
or [JSRL](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/uchendu23a.html) becomes more
attractive once a competent actor and correct logged transitions exist.

There is direct CAGE4 support for a guided hierarchy: [Singh et al.](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2025/papers/Paper77.html)
report learned subpolicies plus an expert mode master outperforming their
decentralized learner, while their learned mode master is weaker. The result
supports learning *within* a stable investigation/recovery framework. Their
100 by 500-step setup and opponent differ from ours. [TERLA](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v299/main/assets/dudman25a/dudman25a.pdf)
shows semantic action reduction and topology transfer on CAGE4, but its
absolute scores and reward setup are not a matched baseline here.

Recent [Optimistic Policy Regularization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06793)
is an interesting later ablation for keeping successful PPO behavior. Its
reported cyber result is on **30-step CAGE2**, and selecting apparently
successful episodes may select easy seeds. Its auxiliary imitation and reward
shaping should be separated before a 400-step CAGE4 claim. [Nyberg and
Johnson](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05775) support entity/host-action
structure and topology transfer on CAGE2; their specialist MLP ensemble is
still stronger in the reported aggregate. Their withheld B-Line attacker is
some attacker-shift evidence, contrary to an older note saying none exists.

## Evaluation standard and kill rules

Use separate training episodes, development validation seeds, and an untouched
final suite. The already examined 7801–7808 suite is development evidence for
future candidates. Keep 8201+ reserved if still untouched, or designate a
new final suite before using those seeds for any selection. Run several
independent training seeds. Compare each candidate with Sleep, random,
round-robin, and the strongest fixed heuristic on the *same* simulator seed,
Red variant, horizon, and native reward. Include Discovery and Finite Red;
report them separately. Record policy/checkpoint hashes and exact source and
configuration versions.

Report paired native-return differences, episode-level uncertainty, host
coverage, maximum/upper-tail scan age, remediation actions and privileged
evaluation outcomes in separate channels. Include the worst episode and
lower-tail return; a modest mean gain with a `-500` failure is not a robust
win. [Agarwal et al., NeurIPS 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264)
motivates interval estimates in small-run RL comparisons. Resample whole
episodes and training runs, not five agents or correlated ticks as if they
were independent. Policy selection may use development simulator rollouts;
if an offline ranker is retained, measure its rank agreement and selection
regret against those rollouts, as suggested by [Active Offline Policy
Selection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10251). A corrected FQE score is
only a candidate selection signal after calibration, never proof of a return
improvement.

Stop a line when its mechanism fails: if masked/validated extraction still
does not preserve sweep coverage across training seeds, change extraction
or data rather than Q iteration count; if contextual scoring does not improve
host selection on held-out episodes, do not scale it to a GNN; if a guided
policy ties the teacher on development and fails on Finite Red, do not claim
generalization. The acceptance claim is a positive paired native-return
effect against the best heuristic on a genuinely untouched suite, without
a material lower-tail or attacker-shift regression.

## Source and contract notes

Only Blue-visible observation history should reach the actor, masks, or
attacker belief. The [history-state critic paper](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2022/pdfs/p44.pdf)
motivates a Blue-history critic under the current contract. It does not
authorize privileged simulator state during training: project instructions
reserve that state for evaluation labels. The [posterior-conditioned CAGE4
paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0004370226001323)
uses a shared belief inferred from joint trajectories; a local-history
version or explicit communication contract is needed for this project.

This audit supersedes the latest `blue-session.md` claim that its six FQE
checkpoints are proven value-indistinguishable. It does not erase the
recorded simulator returns. Earlier proposal status text may still describe
IQL/FQE as fully tested; the numbered findings above explain the narrower
conclusion. The proposed experiments are unrun.
